Cross-Silo Federated Learning for Astronaut Healthcare

Results so far (Phases 3–6). Data: WESAD (terrestrial proxy: chest and wrist signals, 60 s windows, 3 classes: baseline / stress / amusement). Five simulated silos (scenario labels): nasa_earth, esa_analog, iss_commercial, lunar_habitat, mars_transit. The held-out subjects (13, 16, 17; n = 212 windows) are never used in training. Each value is the mean ± std over 5 seeds.

QoI 1 · H1Does federation beat training alone?

MethodThree arms on the same held-out subjects: centralized (pooled, the upper bound), federated (FedAvg, 30 rounds × 1 epoch), and local_only (each silo trains alone, the lower bound).
DataWESAD, partitioned by subject (no subject is in two silos), MLP, 5 seeds.
MetricHeld-out accuracy, macro-F1, AUC.
Finding: federated lands between the bounds. Accuracy: 0.708 vs 0.654 for local-only (4/5 seeds), and macro-F1 is higher in 5/5 seeds. It stays well below centralized (0.868). It never reaches 95% of centralized within 30 rounds.

Right: federated held-out accuracy and macro-F1 by round (mean over 5 seeds). Payload is 21.8 KB per model and 218 KB per round.

QoI 2 · Non-IIDHow does label skew across silos affect FL?

MethodDirichlet(α) label partition, α ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10, ∞}. A quantity-matched control keeps each silo's size but not its skew. The fairness spread is best silo − worst silo.
DataWESAD, same holdout, FedAvg, 5 seeds per α.
MetricFederated macro-F1 and accuracy; per-silo spread.
Finding: federated macro-F1 falls steadily as skew rises (0.668 → 0.218). The quantity-matched control stays flat (0.55–0.63), so label skew causes the drop, not smaller silos. FL still beats the local-only mean at every α. Fairness gets much worse under strong skew: the spread goes from 0.002 to 0.995.

QoI 3 · StrategyDoes FedProx fix non-IID?

MethodFedProx vs FedAvg: matched rounds, seeds and partitions, paired per seed. μ was fixed at 0.01 in advance, and a μ sensitivity sweep was run at α = 0.3.
DataWESAD, Dirichlet α = 0.3, 5 seeds.
MetricPaired Δ accuracy (FedProx − FedAvg).
Finding: no measurable benefit for μ ≤ 0.1 (|Δ| ≤ 0.002). At μ = 1 there is a small, inconsistent gain (+0.016, from 0 to +0.061 by seed). This fits the setup: with 1 local epoch, clients barely drift. FedProx does not fix non-IID here.

QoI 4 · PrivacyCan updates leak data, and what does DP cost?

MethodGradient inversion attack on a single client update from the trained global model (nasa_earth silo). Batch sizes 1, 4 and 32, with and without differential privacy (Opacus, RDP accountant, δ = 0.1/N). ε ∈ {0.5…13}.
DataWESAD, 5 seeds × 3 attack trials. ε is per record (60 s window), not per subject.
MetricRe-identification rate and relative MSE; held-out accuracy vs ε.
Finding: without DP, leakage is real but depends on batch size: 93% of windows re-identified at B = 1, 45% at B = 4, and 15% at B = 32 (≈ 3× chance). Any DP setting pushes the attack to chance. The cost is utility: at ε ≤ 10 the federated model collapses to predicting the majority class (≈ 0.54–0.58 accuracy; majority baseline 0.538).

Left: re-identification rate (dashed line is chance at B = 32 ≈ 0.045). Right: held-out accuracy vs ε (∞ = no DP, plotted at the far right).

QoI 5 · Deep-space commsHow long does it take to reach target accuracy?

MethodSimulated wall-clock (no real sleeping) with per-silo delay, jitter and loss-of-signal windows. Earth, Moon, Mars-near and Mars-far presets. Synchronous FedAvg vs buffered-async FedBuff (K = 3).
DataWESAD by subject, 30 rounds, 5 seeds, loss of signal on.
MetricSimulated time to accuracy 0.673; updates per silo.
Finding: at Mars-far, FedBuff reaches the target 9.5× sooner (1.3 h vs 12.4 h). But the speed comes from leaving silos out: mars_transit contributes 0 updates in every seed. A silo going dark mid-run doesn't crash anything. Without a deadline, sync FedAvg stalls cleanly. With a 1 h deadline, it wastes about 15 h waiting on the dead silo.

Log scale, simulated hours. The sync FedAvg target is reached at the same round under every preset: comms change only the time, not the learning.
